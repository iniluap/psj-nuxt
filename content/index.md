For the past 8 years, I've built interfaces used by
millions of people daily, with a&nbsp;focus on making the web accessible to
everyone.

In 2020, I&nbsp;started working on
[Instapage](https://www.instapage.com/){.block-link},
an enterprise Angular application in the marketing tech field, as
one of the two frontend engineers. Since 2022, I&nbsp;work as
a&nbsp;software engineer at
[GitLab](https://about.gitlab.com/){.block-link}, where
I&nbsp;contribute to the <strong>Source Code Management</strong> group
and lead the <strong>Product Accessibility Working Group</strong>.

My work spans the full development lifecycle - from early ideation and
design collaboration to implementation, testing, and measuring impact in
production. I&nbsp;specialize in <strong>Vue.js</strong>, <strong>digital
accessibility</strong>, and working with <strong>complex legacy codebases</strong>
I'm passionate about well-written and well-organised code, inclusive design,
and ensuring that every feature I&nbsp;build works seamlessly with assistive
technologies.

Before tech, I&nbsp;studied English philology with a&nbsp;focus on
sociolinguistics and taught English as Second Language. I've always
been interested in how language shapes power and perception. I'm
particularly interested in AI&nbsp;discourse: the anthropomorphization in
chatbot interfaces, the promises made by AI&nbsp;companion marketing,
and how narratives about AI&nbsp;"taking over" jobs and the world can overshadow
present-day social issues - like impacts on mental health, labor practices,
and community connection - that we could actually address and mitigate now.

I&nbsp;believe engineers need critical literacy about the social weight of
their technical decisions. The interfaces we build aren't neutral.
They're linguistic and social systems that shape how millions of people
understand themselves and their world.
